**Endpoint → AI services** lists every AI your workforce uses, approved or not,
and what each one is governed by. The page has two tabs:

| Tab | What it lists |
| --- | ------------- |
| **Catalogue** | Every AI service Blindsight recognises, used or not. A decision made here applies from the first request. |
| **Observed** | The recognised AI services your agents have seen on the fleet, with who reached them and how often. |

A workspace where nothing has been seen opens on **Catalogue**. An empty
**Observed** tab reads "No AI traffic observed yet. Agents report each service the
first time it is used." with an **Open the Catalogue** link. On a workspace that
shows only counts it reads "No AI traffic released yet. A service is listed here
once a closed week releases its counts."
 A workspace with
traffic opens on **Observed**. The count beside each tab is the number of rows in
it. Search by host or vendor.

On a workspace that shows only counts, **Observed** lists closed weeks. See
[Who is named in the console](/endpoint/who-is-named).

<Note>
  On a platform that does not serve the Catalogue yet, the page keeps its single
  list without tabs. Nothing is shown as 0 when the platform could not say.
</Note>

## The four decisions

Each row offers the same decisions, on both tabs. They need
`endpoint.ai_services.manage`. Without it the row says **Needs the manage grant**.

| Decision | What it does |
| -------- | ------------ |
| **Approve** | Marks the service as an approved tool. It is no longer [Shadow AI](/endpoint/shadow-ai/overview) and is never blocked by the unapproved-AI setting. **Prompts are still inspected** whenever inspection is on. |
| **Inspect** | Agents read the traffic: personal data is pseudonymized and prompt injection is scored before a prompt leaves the device. Nobody is blocked. |
| **Block** | Enrolled agents deny the connection on their next policy pull. Nothing leaves the device, so there is nothing to inspect. Reversible. |
| **Clear** | Returns the decision to the default. The confirmation says where the service goes back to. |

Some services are also **Managed apps**: a service you onboarded as an app. A
managed app is the strongest decision, so **Approve** and **Block** are off for it.
Remove the app itself to stop managing it.

The console uses the words **Inspect** and **Protect** the other way round from
older pages. **Inspect** is the light posture: nobody is blocked. **Protect** is
the strong one, where a request that trips a rule can be stopped. The next pages
use the console's words.

### Approving keeps inspection on

Approving a service does not switch inspection off. Approve only removes the
service from Shadow AI and from the unapproved-AI block. Blocking is its own
decision. On an approved service, sensitive data is pseudonymized or redacted,
not blocked. A block stays possible for reasons that are not about personal data:
prompt injection, harmful content, or an explicit block.

An approved row reads:

| Row says | Meaning |
| -------- | ------- |
| **Approved, inspected** | Prompts to it are read. |
| **Approved, counted only: this host cannot be intercepted** | Blindsight counts connections to it but cannot read them. |
| **Approved, not inspected** | Nothing reads it. |

A row with no decision reads **Not decided, inspected**, **Not decided, counted
only** or **Not decided** plus what the default does with it.

### What is inspected by default

Built-in services and the most-used AI services (popular ones) are inspected
by default, without anyone approving or deciding. Hugging Face is the exception:
its clients are mostly Python tools that pin certificates, so it is not inspected
until you choose to. Hosts that cannot be intercepted, such as cloud,
identity and operating system update hosts, read **counted only** and the row
says these settings have no effect on them. Less-used services follow later, after
real-device tests.

Approving a service removes it from Shadow AI and keeps it inspected. Services
approved before this release are moved to inspected by the release's data
migration, except those where an admin chose **Stop inspecting**: those keep the
service settings **Off** for pseudonymization and prompt-injection detection.
Approving or inspecting such a service later does not turn the detectors back on.
The row says so: "Approved, but Pseudonymization is Off for this service ...
Approving does not turn it back on: change it here."

### Microsoft 365 Copilot

Copilot is decided as one family. A decision on `copilot.cloud.microsoft` also
covers `m365copilotapp.svc.cloud.microsoft`. The row says **Also covers** and
names the other host. Clear removes the whole family. A rule you set by hand on
one of the family hosts stops the decision with a message that names it.

## Shadow AI shows unapproved services only

The [Shadow AI](/endpoint/shadow-ai/overview) page lists recognised AI that is not
approved. An approved or managed service lives on **AI services**. The figures
on the Shadow AI page are **Unapproved AI**, **Waiting on you**, **High risk** and
**Blocked**. When every recognised service your agents saw is approved, the
page reads **No unapproved AI** and points you to the AI services page.

**Waiting on you** counts services where nothing is decided and nothing is
inspecting them. A page that cannot tell reads **Not available**, not 0.

## Per-service settings: Inherit, On, Off

Two inspection settings can be set for one service:

- **Pseudonymization**: personal data is replaced with placeholders on the device
  before a prompt is sent.
- **Prompt-injection detection**: prompts and replies are scored for injection.

Each has three choices: **Inherit**, **On** and **Off**. **Inherit** is the
default and follows the workspace. **On** switches a setting on for this service
even when the workspace has it off, if your plan and the engine allow it. **Off**
switches it off for this service.

Under each control a line says what is true now and why:

- **On, workspace setting** or **Off, workspace setting**
- **On, set for this service** or **Off, set for this service**
- **On (not available on this plan)**, when On is chosen and nothing can run it
- **On (not active: personal data protection is off)**, for pseudonymization when
  that protection is off for the workspace

A third line reads **Applies on agent 1.7.0 and later: N of M devices**. Older
agents ignore the setting and keep the workspace value, so the count shows how
many devices follow it. If the fleet cannot be counted, the numbers read as a
dash.

### Which setting wins

The first setting that is not **Inherit** wins, in this order:

1. The policy's setting for this service.
2. The service's own setting.
3. The policy's default.
4. The workspace setting.

The service's own setting beats the policy default. Where two policies disagree
for one person, **On** wins. A policy that blocks a service still blocks it.
The effective line names the source: **On, workspace setting**, **On, set for this
service**, **On, policy default**, or **On, from policy Finance**.

Changing a service's setting needs `endpoint.ai_services.manage`. A person
without the permission sees the rows read-only: "Changing this needs the manage
grant." Each Catalogue and Observed row opens an inspection panel for the host
that governs it, with these controls and the line "Policies can change this for
the people they reach."

The service's setting applies to the host whose rule governs the service. A
setting on `example.com` covers the subdomains its rule covers. A custom service
with an exact host covers that host only. A host no rule governs is refused.

### Settings in a policy

A policy has an **Inspection** section, marked "applies on agent 1.7.0 and
later". It holds:

- **Default for every service in this policy**: **Pseudonymization** and
  **Prompt-injection detection**, each **Inherit**, **On** or **Off**. A default
  applies to inspected services, not to blocked or tunnelled ones.
- A list of services, each with its own pair of settings for that policy.

A folded section shows a summary such as "Default: prompt-injection detection on.
2 services are set on their own." The save bar lists each change. Saving
inspection settings needs `endpoint.protections.configure`, which an admin holds
by default. Without it the section is read-only, and a save that changes it is
refused with a message naming the permission. The rest of the policy is not saved
in that case either.

### Works council workspaces

In a workspace with the works council module on, switching prompt-injection
detection **On** is refused until the works council notice flow exists, whether
from a policy or for one service. Admins see:

> This workspace has a works council. Prompt-injection detection cannot be
> switched on until the notice flow exists; ask support. An On already stored
> stays, and the policy can be saved with it.

In the policy editor the **On** choice for prompt-injection detection is
disabled, with the hint "Needs the notice flow in a works-council workspace",
except where that exact On is already stored. For a service, the change is refused
with `422 works_council_notice_required`. **Off**, **Inherit**, pseudonymization
**On** and saving an unchanged value are never refused.

### Older agents

Per-service and per-policy settings take effect only on agent 1.7.0 and later. The
console says "Applies on agent 1.7.0 and later: N of M devices" and never claims
coverage on an older agent, which keeps the workspace value.

## Permissions

| Permission | Lets the user |
| ---------- | ------------- |
| `endpoint.discovery.view` | See the Catalogue, the Observed list and a service's settings. |
| `endpoint.ai_services.manage` | Approve, inspect, block and clear, and change a service's settings. |
| `endpoint.protections.configure` | Change the **Inspection** section of a policy. |
| `endpoint.ai_services.add` | [Add a custom AI service](/endpoint/custom-ai-services). |
| `endpoint.ai_services.teach` | Teach a custom service's request format. |
