Some sites dress up as a well-known AI service to collect sign-ins or to push a
fake desktop app. They are **impersonating sites**, also called **lookalike**
sites or **fake AI sites**. Their front pages often say exactly what the real
service says, so the evidence that would normally confirm an AI service would
confirm the fake one too. Blindsight therefore asks a different question first:
not "is this AI", but "is this pretending to be an AI service it is not".

Every host your fleet reaches is checked against the names of the major AI
services before anything else is decided about it. This page covers what
happens when one matches, what is blocked, and what you can change.

## What Blindsight does with a lookalike

A host that matches is listed on **Endpoint → Shadow AI** as **Impersonates**
*service*, for example *Impersonates ChatGPT: the real service is chatgpt.com*.
From then on:

- It is **never treated as an AI service**. It is not counted in the Shadow AI
  figures, on the Overview's **What changed**, in report findings, or in **In
  use**, **Observed**, **Inspected**, **Approved** and **Waiting** on the **AI
  services** page.
- It ranks **High** risk, with the real service named.
- It is never approved or acted on by the **Newly discovered AI** setting, and
  never suggested for the shared catalogue.
- It raises the **Impersonating site reached** alert, once per site.
- **Approve** is unavailable on its row. The row tells you which domain to
  approve if people need the real service.

The real service is never flagged. A brand's own domains, every host in the
built-in catalogue and every built-in rule are never matched.

## How a lookalike is recognised

Expand the row to see which check matched.

| Check shown | What it means |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| Spelled with lookalike characters | Letters from another alphabet, or digits, that look like the service's letters. |
| One letter away from the brand | A misspelling of the service's name. |
| The brand name on a domain ending the brand does not own | For example the service's name under a different country or generic ending. |
| The brand name beside a download or sign-in word | For example *desktop*, *download*, *login* or *free* next to the name. |
| Uses the brand's own domain as part of another site's name | The real domain at the start of someone else's address. |
| Named an imitation in the Blindsight catalogue | Blindsight's own review recorded the site as an imitation. |

Only the host name is read. Blindsight does not publish its list of service names
or its matching rules.

## Which lookalikes are blocked

Blocking is a setting of your workspace, and it only touches the clearest cases:

- a site spelled with lookalike characters
- a download or sign-in word beside the service's name
- a near miss of the name, or the name on a different ending, when other
  evidence supports it, such as how recently the domain was registered

Every other lookalike is **named and alerted, never blocked**. A site you decided
about yourself is never touched by this setting.

**Block impersonating sites** is **on** for workspaces created from 16 November
2026. It is **off** for a workspace that existed before, until an administrator
turns it on. There are two places to do it:

- the notice that says how many sites your people reached that pretend to be a
  well-known AI service, with **Turn on blocking**. It shows at the top of every
  page of the console, to the people who can edit the Default policy, and stays
  until you act on it or close it.
- **Endpoint → Policies → Default policy**, the **Block impersonating sites** switch

A workspace that has blocking on without ever having chosen it is told, while
the block holds hosts, that blocking is on and where to turn it off. Every
change is written to the [audit trail](/compliance/audit-trail).

### Block or warn

Under **When a person opens one**, choose what a device does with a site the rule
covers:

- **Block** refuses the site.
- **Warn** shows a page saying the site is not the service it imitates, with a
  link to the real service. The person may continue anyway, and the site then
  opens on that one device for a short time. Each continue is recorded and
  counted on the row (*Opened N times past the warning in the last 30 days*).

**Warn** needs desktop 2.1.8 or later. An older desktop app blocks. Moving from
**Block** to **Warn** weakens a protection, so it is recorded at high severity
in the audit trail and raises the policy-weakened alert. A site you blocked
yourself is always refused.

## What people see

- A person who is blocked sees a block page that names the service the site
  copies and where the real one is (desktop 2.1.7 and later). Workspaces can add
  a short message and a contact to it; see
  [Block page message and contact](/endpoint/policies#block-page-message-and-contact).
- In a workspace that names people, each person who reached a **blocked**
  lookalike gets one plain email, once per site, naming the site, the service it
  imitates and the real address. It tells them to report it if they typed
  anything there. A weaker lookalike is never emailed, and a workspace that shows
  totals only sends nothing.

## What to do with one

1. Open the row on **Endpoint → Shadow AI** and read which check matched.
2. If blocking is off, choose **Block** on the row, or turn on **Block
   impersonating sites** for all of them.
3. Check the devices and people listed against it. Anyone who reached it may have
   entered work credentials or run a download.
4. If people need the real service, approve its real domain.

### Not a lookalike

If a site is listed by mistake, choose **Not a lookalike** on its row. It is
offered on rows nobody has decided about yet. Devices then stop treating the
site as a copy of that service. The site is not blocked and not read: this is a
record of your decision, not an approval, and the row reads **Not a lookalike**
rather than **Approved**. Each decision is written to the audit trail.

## The alert

**Impersonating site reached** is a high-severity alert sent once per site. Choose
it at **Endpoint → Configurations → Alerts** like any other alert. In a
workspace that shows totals only, the alert names the site and nothing about who
reached it or when.
