---
title: "MSP accounts"
description: "What an MSP account is, how it differs from an organization workspace, what its clients see, and the security model behind opening one."
---

An MSP account is a workspace of type `msp`. It runs no product of its
own: there are no datasets, no scans, no firewall apps that belong to
the account itself. What it holds is a licence stating how many client
workspaces it may create and a pool of seats it shares out among them,
and the tools to create, license, and work inside those clients.

Every client is an ordinary organization workspace with its own people,
its own roles, and its own data. Nobody from the account becomes a
member of a client. Instead, an account member **opens** a client for a
limited window, works inside it as a fixed, restricted role, and
leaves.

## How this differs from an organization workspace

| | Organization workspace | MSP account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Runs its own product | Yes | No |
| Has its own people doing the work | Yes | No, its clients do |
| Menu | The product sidebar (Runtime Security, Data Security, DLP, Compliance...) | A purpose-built menu: Portfolio and Operations |
| What you see when you sign in | Your own workspace's data | A roster of clients and their state, never a client's content |

The account's own menu is:

- **Portfolio**: **Overview** (what needs a person today, across every
  client) and **Clients** (every client, its protection, licence and
  your access).
- **Operations**: **Billing** (the seat pool and what renews when),
  **Team** (this account's own people, roles, keys and directory), and
  **Settings**.

<Note>
  A client workspace never sees anything extra by having an MSP. Its
  own console looks exactly like any other organization's, except for
  the **Your MSP** setting described in [For clients](/msp/for-clients).
</Note>

## What clients see

Nothing changes for a client's own people day to day. The one visible
difference is the **Your MSP** setting. The operator working inside the
workspace sees a band naming the account, the time left and a way to
leave; the client's own people do not. Sessions show in the client's
audit trail. See [Opening a client](/msp/opening-a-client).

## The security model, in one list

- An operator opens a client for a limited window at a time, never by
  becoming a member of it.
- Opening needs a fresh two-factor code from the operator's own
  authenticator app, unless the client has switched that requirement
  off for itself.
- Everything an operator changes inside a client is recorded in the
  **client's own audit trail** before the change runs. A change that
  cannot be recorded is refused rather than made silently.
- Operators can never, from inside a client: invite, create, edit or
  remove people, assign roles, reset anyone's password or two-factor,
  create API keys, configure directory or SSO, change the security
  policy, decide whether the MSP itself may have access, or mint a
  credential that would outlive the session. They also cannot change
  their own account while a client is open. That lives with the MSP
  account, and is reached by leaving first.
- The client's own admins decide, at any time, whether their MSP may
  open the workspace at all. Turning it off ends every session already
  open, immediately.
