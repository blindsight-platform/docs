An MSP account's own people, roles, and invitations live on **Team**
(`/access`), the same Access management surface every workspace uses.
What is specific to an MSP account is the `msp.clients.*` permission
group, the `msp.billing.view` permission, and the fixed role an account
member runs under once they open a client.

## The permissions

| Permission | Lets the holder |
| --- | --- |
| `msp.clients.view` | See the account's client workspaces, the seats each holds, and how much of the account's allowance is used. |
| `msp.clients.manage` | Create client workspaces, invite each one's first admin, and move seats and licence between clients within the account's allowance. |
| `msp.clients.access` | Work inside a client workspace for a limited time, after a fresh two-factor code (unless the client switched that off). Everything done there is recorded in the client's audit trail. Nothing that grants lasting access to the client can be done this way. |
| `msp.clients.people` | Inside a client you opened, see its people, their roles, invitations and access activity. Changing any of it stays with the client's own admins. |

| `msp.billing.view` | Read [Billing](/msp/portfolio-and-billing#billing) and its CSV, and nothing else in the account. |

These are granted through custom roles exactly like any other
permission: see [Access management](/administration/access).

## The billing-only role

`msp-billing` is a built-in role that holds `msp.billing.view` and
nothing else. It exists only in an MSP account: in an organization
workspace it is not listed and cannot be assigned, invited or mapped
from a directory group. Give it to someone, such as a finance contact,
who needs the numbers but not the clients.

A member with only this role signs in to **Billing**. It is the only
item in their menu, **Settings** is hidden, and every other page of the
account answers with a refusal. Anyone who holds `msp.clients.view`
can read Billing too.

## Limiting an operator to some clients

By default every member of the account sees every client. To limit an
operator:

1. Put [tags](/msp/clients#tags) on your clients.
2. Open **Client access** (Operations → Client access, `/msp/operators`).
   It lists the account's people with their roles and what each can open.
3. Choose a person, turn off **See every client**, and enter the tags
   in **Limit to tags**, separated by commas.

A scoped operator sees and opens only clients that carry at least one
of their tags. This applies to Overview, Clients, Billing and its CSV,
Activity, and opening a client. A client outside the scope behaves as if
it does not exist.

- **Admins are never scoped.** An admin or owner always sees every
  client, so an account cannot lock itself out of its own clients.
- **A scoped member cannot change scopes**, their own included.
  Otherwise the way out of a scope would be open to the person it
  limits.
- **A scope fails closed.** If no client carries any of a person's tags
  any more, for example after the last one was released, they see no
  clients.
- Seeing the page needs `users.view`. Changing a scope needs
  `users.assign_roles`. A scope needs at least one tag, or **See every
  client**. Each change is recorded.

## Two-factor is required to open a client

`msp.clients.access` is not enough on its own. Opening any client also
needs two-factor authentication turned on for your **own** account,
checked fresh every time. Without it, the open dialog sends you to
your profile to turn it on.

## Inside a client: a fixed operator role

Once a session is open, you run inside that client as a fixed role,
**not** your usual role in the account and not a role either side
configures. It is every permission an admin holds, minus what would
outlive the session or take the workspace away from the people who own
it:

- Inviting, creating, editing or removing people, assigning roles,
  resetting anyone's password, two-factor, or approving an email
  change.
- Revoking or sending invitations.
- Managing roles, API keys, or directory and SSO.
- Changing the security policy or licence settings.
- `billing.checkout`.
- Anything under `msp.clients.*` itself: an operator working inside a
  client cannot use that session to reach into the MSP account, or into
  another client.

This set is fixed on purpose: neither the account nor the client edits
it, and no role configuration is read for it.

### Seeing the client's people

Seeing who the client's people are is gated separately, by
`msp.clients.people` on your account. Without it, an operator session
holds none of `users.view`, `users.view_activity`, or
`invitations.view` inside the client: the roster is simply not there.

With it, the roster is read-only. A client the MSP created shows its
people to holders of `msp.clients.people`. A workspace that linked
chose at approval, and if it chose to hide them, nobody at the account
sees them.

Without people access an operator does not see names. The client's
people and devices appear as coded identities: `Person` or `Device`
followed by a six-character code, so the work can go on without naming
anyone. A few
surfaces cannot be coded and are refused: people search, the member
list, the roles page (which names who holds each role), and rollout
actions that take an email address. The audit trail can be read, but
not searched by text or filtered by person, and its signed export is
refused.

### What never runs inside a client, for anyone

- **API keys.** A key authenticates without the person behind the
  cookie being present, so a key's request always runs in the key's
  own workspace, never a client's.
- **Your own account.** Changing your password, two-factor, email or
  profile is refused while a client is open: that belongs to the MSP
  account. Leave the client first.
- **Minting the client's audit webhook token**, a credential that
  would outlive the session and write into the very trail that is
  supposed to be recording you.
- **Creating a Runtime Security App token.** Like an API key, a token
  works without anyone present in the client, so only the client's own
  admins can create one. Enrollment tokens for devices are not
  affected.
- **The client's own decision about its MSP.** Only the client's
  admins can switch access on or off, or change the code and duration
  settings, never an operator working inside it.
